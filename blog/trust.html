how do we trust eachother so much?

Just this last Saturday I had my car broken into overnight, while it was parked only 75 feet from my apartment. I had parked it in that same spot many times before, not worrying about how my car could become a target for someone looking for a quick profit. I left a few blankets, books I liked to read on roadtrips, a pack of cards, a car recovery kit, and a tiny (very cheap) toolset in the back, which was missing the cold morning I found it. Choosing to park there wasn't evidently reckless or anything, it's just that I trusted the amount of times I had previously returned to an unmolested vehicle, as well as the greater reputation of the area I was in. Clearly, I now feel very different. Apart from feeling pretty violated and angry, I felt sort of stupid because of decision. Nonetheless, while I was vacuuming the broken glass from my backseat, I thought about a few things.

Firstly, I thought about my perception of the 'reputation' my block had. I live fairly close to the police precinct, as well as many buildings that are spattered with cameras, so I assumed that people would be reluctant to commit a serious (can you hear the air quotes..?) crime. I really trusted that those deterrents would be enough, but none of them seemed to make a difference when I saw a small portion of my personality rifled through and cast all over the street. I trusted that the people around me, strangers really, would see my stuff and beleive there was a person behind it. Not just the idea, but a real person who would feel shitty when his stuff was taken, when he paid the nearly $1000 bill to replace the smashed windows, and when he realized the area he told his friends was 'safe' really wasn't. It makes me think more about how, as humans living among other humans, how we can really look at someone else and feel empathetic towards what is going on in their life. There are few people in our life we can talk with and feel even half as deeply as they do about the things they care about. So, how should I expect someone who is desperate for a warm blanket, or even their next bottle of alcohol, to see the opportunity for a quick $20 and feel the pain behind all of the recourse I have as a result? It seems silly to me now.

Though - its not to say that we should look at every person with contempt and distrust because we know they can never feel like we feel. I think its more of a lesson in how we should treat those who we know do care about even the smallest things we care about. I may be trying to justify my feelings though.
